Privacy Policy

Last updated: 4 August 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how LOGDit ("we", "us", "our") collects, uses, stores and protects your information when you use our mobile application and related services (the "Service"). We are committed to handling your data in line with the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018.

1. Who we are

LOGDit is operated by LOGDIT LTD (trading as "LOGDit"), a company registered in England and Wales (company number 17215461), registered office: 39 Wallington Way, Frome BA11 3NF, United Kingdom. We are the data controller for personal data described in this policy.

ICO registration number: ZC145494.

We have not appointed a Data Protection Officer. For all privacy matters, including data subject requests and complaints, please contact privacy@logdit.app.

Our role

LOGDit is not a clinical record system and is not intended to process patient data. Patient identifiers are treated as information to be removed, not retained or used. We use your content only to provide the Service to you - never to build patient records and never for any purpose of our own. We do not act as a data processor on behalf of you, your employer, or any NHS body. You must not input patient-identifying data (see section 15 and our Terms of Service).

2. What we collect

Account information you provide

  • Email address (used as your account identifier and to send sign-in codes)
  • Name
  • Medical specialty (e.g. General Practice) and training stage (e.g. ST1, ST3) - used to load the right curriculum framework
  • A one-time passcode (OTP) we email to you to verify sign-in

Your email address, name, specialty and training stage are needed to create and operate your account - without them we cannot provide the Service. Everything else you put into the app is optional and entirely up to you.

Content you create in the app

  • Voice recordings you make when logging a case
  • Transcripts derived from those recordings (see section 6 for how patient identifiers are redacted)
  • AI-generated draft reflections and capability tags, and your edits to them - including the entry text and your own-words capability justifications
  • Titles you give to your entries and conversations
  • Answers you give to in-app questions, including structured (single- or multi-choice) selections and free-text replies in the conversation
  • Notes you attach to an entry, and any self-assessment rating and review comments you add
  • PDP goals you keep or create, together with the actions, intended evidence, deadlines and completion reviews you add to them
  • Review periods you set up (name and dates)
  • Earlier versions of your entries, kept as version history when you edit or restore them

Technical and device data we collect automatically

  • App version, device type and operating system (sent as headers with each request)
  • Session identifiers, an approximate device label (e.g. "iPhone, iOS 18") and the time each session was last used, for managing your signed-in devices
  • IP address and browser/app user-agent - used for security, rate-limiting and abuse prevention, and recorded alongside your consent acknowledgements as a record of the choices you made and when
  • Request identifiers and server request logs (the endpoint, method, response status, your IP address, your browser/app user-agent and your account identifier). These are sent to our logging and performance-monitoring provider (see section 8), used for debugging and operational monitoring, and deleted automatically after a short retention period (see section 7). Authentication tokens and cookies are redacted from these logs, and we do not send the content of your entries, recordings or transcripts to that provider
  • Usage events - a record of certain in-app actions (an event type, timestamp and limited metadata, linked to your account) used for usage limits, abuse prevention and aggregate product analysis. These are automatically deleted after 90 days
  • Technical metadata about your uploads, recordings and transcription
  • Crash reports and error diagnostics via our error-monitoring provider (see section 8). We do not send your name, email, content or authentication tokens to that provider.

What we do not collect

We do not knowingly collect identifiable patient data. Our pipeline removes patient identifiers (such as names, dates of birth, NHS numbers, addresses and postcodes) from transcripts before they are used to draft your portfolio entry. See section 6 for the full pipeline, including which sub-processors handle your audio and transcript at each stage. We do not collect your location, contacts, photos, calendar, browser history or advertising identifiers. We do not use third-party advertising or cross-site tracking.

The app asks for permission to use your microphone so you can record a voice note. Recording only happens when you tap to record. We do not record in the background. You can revoke microphone permission at any time in your device settings; you will then be unable to use the voice recording feature.

  • To provide and operate the Service (transcription, redaction, drafting entries, exporting PDFs)
  • To create and authenticate your account, including sending OTP sign-in codes
  • To improve the Service (aggregated and de-identified usage analysis, crash diagnostics)
  • To communicate service updates, security notices and (with your consent) product news

5. Legal bases for processing (UK GDPR Article 6)

  • Contract - to deliver the Service to you under our Terms of Service (account creation, authentication, transcription, drafting, storage of your entries).
  • Legitimate interests - to keep the Service secure, prevent fraud, monitor errors and improve the product. We balance these interests against your rights and freedoms; you can object at any time (see section 11).
  • Consent - for optional marketing communications and any feature you explicitly opt into. You can withdraw consent at any time without affecting the lawfulness of earlier processing.
  • Legal obligation - where we are required to retain or disclose information by applicable law.

Patient confidentiality and sensitive information

Your reflections are about your professional development and your clinical practice - not about your own health, and not about any identifiable patient. The app is built to keep it that way. Before any transcript is used to draft your entry, our redaction pipeline (see section 6) removes patient identifiers such as names, dates of birth, NHS numbers, addresses and postcodes. This is what lets you reflect freely on real cases: the clinical detail that matters for your learning stays, and the details that identify a patient do not. Keeping identifying and other sensitive information out of what we store - the UK GDPR calls these categories "special category data" (Article 9) - is a deliberate design choice.

What we ask of you. Please avoid including details that identify a patient, and avoid recording information about your own health, racial or ethnic origin, religious or philosophical beliefs or sexual orientation. We never ask you for any of this, and we do not use your content to build a profile of you. A good reflection does not need the patient's identity - what counts for your portfolio is what you did, what you learned, and what you would do differently.

If something slips through. No automated redaction is perfect (see section 15). If identifying or sensitive information is captured despite these safeguards, it is incidental and unintended. We do not use it for any purpose beyond providing the Service to you, we do not share it with your supervisor, deanery, employer or anyone else (see section 10), and you can edit or delete any entry - or your whole account - at any time (see sections 7 and 11).

Your choice. The Service cannot operate without AI transcription, redaction and drafting, so there is no option to turn those off. If you would prefer your data not to be processed in the way this policy describes, you can choose not to create an account, and you can close your account at any time.

6. PII redaction and AI processing

Your reflection is processed by external speech-to-text and language-model sub-processors (listed on our sub-processors page) to produce a transcript, redact patient identifiers and draft your portfolio entry.

Before any text is used to draft your entry, it passes through automated redaction that removes both structured identifiers (NHS numbers, National Insurance numbers, email addresses, UK phone numbers, UK postcodes, payment card numbers and labelled dates of birth) and contextual identifiers (personal names, organisation names and place names that appear alongside specific persons).

What this means for your data. Because transcription and redaction are themselves automated processes, your audio and the raw transcript briefly transit our sub-processors in unredacted form before the redacted version is produced. The audio recording itself is only sent to the speech-to-text processor; it is never sent to a language model. Our sub-processors operate under Data Processing Agreements that restrict them to providing the service to us and require them to retain your content only for the time needed to return a result - our transcription provider deletes its copy of your audio and transcript within 24 hours.

No sub-processor uses your content to train AI or machine learning models. For most of them this is a prohibition written into their data processing agreement. The exception is our transcription provider, whose standard terms permit model training by default: we have opted out of their model improvement programme, and we did so before the Service carried any real user content. See our sub-processors page.

Abuse monitoring. Our language-model provider operates automated abuse monitoring across its AI services. Text flagged by that system may be stored for a limited period and reviewed by authorised staff at that provider. Only redacted text is ever sent to this stage - your audio and raw transcript are not.

Automated decision-making (UK GDPR Article 22)

Our AI generates draft reflections, suggested capability tags and proposed PDP goals. These are drafts only. They have no legal or similarly significant effect on you. Nothing is submitted to your eportfolio, training programme director or deanery automatically. You review, edit and approve every entry before exporting or using it. You can always start from a blank entry and ignore the AI suggestions.

7. Data storage and retention

  • Personal data is stored on cloud infrastructure in the United Kingdom and the European Economic Area, encrypted in transit (TLS) and at rest. Specific storage locations and providers are listed on our sub-processors page (see section 8).
  • Audio recordings are stored encrypted. Your recording is deleted from our storage when you delete the message or entry it belongs to, or when you close your account. Our transcription provider deletes its own copy of the audio and transcript within 24 hours.
  • Transcripts, reflections, capability tags and PDP goals are retained while your account is active. You can delete any individual entry at any time from inside the app.
  • One-time sign-in codes (OTPs) expire after 5 minutes and are automatically deleted shortly afterwards.
  • Sign-in sessions expire after 90 days of inactivity and are then deleted.
  • Account closure - when you request account deletion there is a 48-hour grace period during which you can cancel. After that, all your content is permanently deleted and your account record is anonymised, except where we are required to retain limited records (for example, for accounting, tax or legal dispute purposes) for the period set by law.
    Account deletion covers your database records and your stored files. It does not reach the technical logs described below, which are held by our monitoring providers on their own fixed schedules and expire automatically. Those logs contain request metadata - such as your IP address, user-agent and account identifier - and not the content of your entries.
  • Server request logs and performance monitoring data are retained by our logging provider for up to 30 days then deleted.
  • Usage events (a record of certain in-app actions, used for usage limits, abuse prevention and aggregate product analysis) are automatically deleted after 90 days.
  • Crash and error logs are retained by our error-monitoring provider for up to 90 days then deleted.

9. International transfers

Your data is stored on cloud infrastructure in the United Kingdom and the European Economic Area, both of which provide an adequate level of protection for personal data under UK law (UK→EEA adequacy regulations 2021). However, some of our providers are United States companies, or can reach the data from outside the UK and EEA even where it is stored here - for example, to provide technical support. Those are international transfers.

The categories of provider involved are speech-to-text transcription, language-model processing, database and cloud storage, error monitoring and server logging, and transactional email delivery. Each is named, with its location, on our sub-processors page.

Two of these are worth stating plainly. Our transcription provider is a United States company, and it is the one stage that receives your audio recording before patient identifiers have been removed. Our language-model provider stores data in the United Kingdom, but the processing itself may take place in any of its regions worldwide - that stage receives only redacted text.

Safeguards. Every one of these transfers is covered by a recognised legal safeguard: depending on the provider, the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, the UK International Data Transfer Agreement or Addendum, Binding Corporate Rules, or the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Several are covered by more than one. A copy of the safeguards applying to any particular provider is available on request at privacy@logdit.app.

Supplementary measures. Alongside those legal mechanisms we rely on technical and contractual protections. The most important is the order of our pipeline: patient identifiers are removed from your transcript before it reaches any language model, so the stage with the widest geographic reach receives only redacted text. Our providers are contractually restricted to providing the service to us, none of them uses your content to train AI models (see section 6), and retention is limited by contract or by configuration - our transcription provider deletes your audio and transcript within 24 hours. Where a provider receives a government request for your data, our agreements require them to challenge overbroad orders, disclose the minimum necessary, and notify us where they are legally permitted to do so.

Note that your audio and the raw transcript transit our transcription provider in unredacted form before the redacted version exists, as described in section 6. That is an unavoidable consequence of transcription being automated, and it is why we ask you not to record patient-identifying information in the first place (see section 15).

A summary of our Transfer Impact Assessment is available on request at privacy@logdit.app.

15. Patient information

Information about other people in your recordings

When you record a reflection about a case, it may contain information about a patient, a colleague or another person, even where you have not named them. Those people are not our users, and because our pipeline is designed to strip identifying details we have no way to contact them. Publishing this section is how we make that information available to them. If you are reading this because you think you may have been described in someone's reflection, this is our commitment to you.

  • We never build a record of any patient. Information about another person exists only as part of the author's own reflection. It is never separated out, indexed, linked to a named individual, or used to create a profile of anyone.
  • Identifiers are removed automatically. Before a transcript is used to draft an entry, our redaction pipeline removes names, dates of birth, NHS numbers, addresses, postcodes and similar identifiers (see section 6).
  • The unredacted version is short-lived. Audio and the raw transcript reach our transcription provider before redaction has taken place, and that provider deletes its copy within 24 hours (see sections 7 and 9).
  • Nothing is disclosed. We do not share entries with the author's employer, supervisor, deanery, the GMC or anyone else (see section 10), and no provider we use may train AI models on them (see section 6).
  • It is deleted along with the entry. When an author deletes an entry or closes their account, anything it contained about another person is deleted with it (see section 7).

If you believe information about you has been included in a reflection on LOGDit and you wish to exercise your data protection rights, contact privacy@logdit.app. We should be straightforward with you about a limit here: because entries are de-identified by design, we may be genuinely unable to find information relating to any particular person. Where we cannot identify you from the data we hold, Article 11 of the UK GDPR means we may be unable to act on an access, correction or deletion request. We will always explain the position rather than simply decline.

Your responsibility as a user

When you record a case, you are responsible for ensuring that your recording complies with your professional, employer and information-governance obligations (including GMC Confidentiality: good practice in handling patient information and any NHS Trust or practice policy that applies to you). Anything you record is transcribed and processed in unredacted form before identifiers are removed, so any patient identifiers you include will reach our processors; our redaction pipeline removes them before they are used to draft your entry, but is not infallible and does not prevent them reaching the transcription and processing steps. Please do not record in clinical environments where doing so would breach local policy.
